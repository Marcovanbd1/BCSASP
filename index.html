About the programme

Gentle, adaptable and welcoming

Sessions combine gentle movement, focused attention, relaxation and simple breathing practices. The approach is adapted to the people in the room.

It is not a fast fitness class and you will not be asked to force your body into difficult positions.

Small group • Six sessions • NHS referral
Places are limited to 15 people. To get the most from the programme, please plan to attend the full course.

What to expect

  • A welcoming group of up to 15 people.
  • Six guided sessions, with practices developing gradually.
  • Gentle movement, relaxation, attention and simple breathing practices.
  • Choices and adjustments rather than one fixed routine for everyone.
  • Time to notice what is helpful and ask questions.
  • Ideas for safe practice between sessions, if you wish.

Yoga is more than exercise

Yoga has a long tradition of bringing together movement, attention, reflection and ways of living well. Some people come mainly for safe exercise. Others value the quiet, meditative side, or find that the practice gives them space to reflect on what matters in life. You can take part whatever your beliefs; there is no religious requirement.

The practices are taught in clear, everyday language. Everything is optional and can be adjusted.

Why learn with a teacher?

A general online video cannot see how you are moving or respond to pain, health conditions or confidence. In these sessions, a trained teacher can explain the practice, offer safer alternatives and help you find an approach that suits you.

This matters particularly when you are new to yoga or living with a long-term health condition.

Who may benefit?

The programme may suit adults who want to:

Move more comfortably and build confidence in movement.

Remain active while living with persistent pain.

Improve strength, balance, mobility or general fitness.

Feel calmer and manage day-to-day stress.

Support sleep, energy or concentration.

Learn practical skills they can continue at home.

Take part in a supportive small group.

Yoga affects people differently. It may help some people, but it is not a cure and it does not replace medical assessment, treatment, physiotherapy or psychological support.

Is the programme suitable for me?

Safe participation comes first

Before you begin

For this course, you must be able to get down to the floor and back up again independently.

Please tell the referring clinician and the teacher about any health condition, recent injury, operation, pregnancy, dizziness, balance problem or other concern that may affect safe participation.

During sessions

If a movement causes sharp pain, makes symptoms clearly worse or feels unsafe, stop and tell the teacher.

You will be encouraged to work within a comfortable range and rest whenever needed.

Benefits emerging from the questionnaires

In the current matched feedback from 10 participants, the clearest positive patterns were in:

  • Confidence in dealing with challenges
  • Daytime energy
  • Sleep
  • Emotional wellbeing
  • Feeling calmer and less distracted
  • Ability to focus
  • Connection with other people

Changes in physical discomfort and overall physical health were smaller and mixed.

This is an early service evaluation, not a clinical trial. The group is small, there is no comparison group and the questionnaire is based on self-report. The findings are encouraging, but they do not prove that yoga caused the changes.

Evidence

What the evidence says

Movement and persistent pain

NICE recommends supervised group exercise for adults with chronic primary pain, adapted to individual needs, abilities and preferences.

Low back pain

A 2022 Cochrane review found that yoga may produce small improvements in pain and back-related function compared with no exercise. Benefits were similar to other back-focused exercise, and the average differences were small.

Stress, sleep and emotional wellbeing

Research suggests yoga may support stress management, sleep and mental or emotional wellbeing for some people, but results vary and the evidence is not equally strong for every outcome or health condition.

Meditation and focused attention

Yoga can include quiet attention and meditation as well as movement. These practices may help some people notice thoughts and physical tension without reacting to them immediately. They are skills to practise, not a guaranteed treatment.

Safety and teaching

Yoga is generally considered safe when it is taught appropriately. A qualified teacher can adapt practices and help reduce avoidable strain, particularly for beginners and people with health conditions.
